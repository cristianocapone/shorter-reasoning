**Replaying the shortest verified solution helps a model finish within its token cap.** **a**, Identical copies of each model are fine-tuned (LoRA r32, 1 epoch) on one of its own verified solutions per training problem: the shortest (S), a random one (R) or the longest (L); the untouched model is the control. **b**, Accuracy of shortest replay minus random (vs R) and minus longest replay (vs L) on ARC-AGI-2 (Qwen3.8-27B program-writing agent, 72 evaluation puzzles, 5 seeds per arm; puzzles × 100/72 gives percentage points of puzzles solved), MATH-500 (Qwen3-4B, 5 seeds per arm; DeepSeek-R1-Distill-Llama-8B, 3 seeds per arm) and APPS (Qwen3-4B, 3 seeds per arm), per-sample accuracy points otherwise (the units are not comparable across settings): dots are mean differences and bars paired-bootstrap 95% intervals over problems, filled when the interval excludes 0. Shortest replay has the highest accuracy of the three rules in every setting. Pooled over the four settings (Stouffer's method on the one-sided bootstrap tails), z = 3.16 against random replay (P = 0.002) and z = 5.73 against longest replay (P < 0.001), two-sided. **c**, Because accuracy = P(finish within the token cap) × P(correct | finished), the paired difference between shortest replay and random or longest replay splits exactly into a finishing term, ΔF × mean Q, and a quality term, ΔQ × mean F (means over the two arms; intervals are the bootstrap intervals of ΔF and ΔQ times the same factors; caps 8k tokens per MATH sample and 24k per ARC agent message): the whole gain comes from finishing, and finished answers are not better. The ARC rows come from the agent's message logs, where restart cost is the tokens spent per code-carrying message (untouched agent, whose message logs cover 3 of its 5 seeds: 61.3% cut, 54.9k; S vs untouched two-sided P = 0.19 and 0.12).
